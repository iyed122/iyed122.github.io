Case study · ATEME

An AI assistant that knows what your engineering team knows

Built at TELNET Holding for ATEME, the French video-delivery company · 2026

1:49 demo: an answer with its sources, the evaluation report, training runs, the release gate holding a model back until the evidence was strong enough, and the model in production.

The problem

An engineering team's knowledge lives in three places: tickets in Jira, documentation in Confluence, code and merge requests in GitLab. Finding an answer meant searching each one, or asking the colleague who knew. When that colleague is busy, new hires wait. When that colleague leaves, the knowledge leaves too.

What I built

One assistant that searches all three at once, answers in English or French, and cites the ticket, page or merge request behind every claim. It runs on the company's own servers, so internal data never goes to an outside AI service. A new model version only goes live after it beats the current one on a fixed test.

The software around it: a React interface that streams answers as they are written, a FastAPI backend, integrations with the Jira, Confluence and GitLab REST APIs, and Docker for deployment.

What it changed

  • ATEME bought it, and TELNET paid me a share of the sale.
  • About 20 minutes saved per engineer per day.
  • It started with individual engineers, then spread department by department.
  • Onboarding got easier in every department: a new engineer can ask the assistant before asking a colleague.
  • What one engineer knew stays searchable after they move on.

What 20 minutes a day is worth: about 9 working days per engineer per year. For a team of 50 engineers, that is roughly 460 working days a year, the time of two full-time engineers given back to the team.

How it works, for engineers
  • Data: 428,593 tickets and 47,851 pages, cut into 1.28 M segments and linked by 1.8 M+ typed relations in Neo4j.
  • Retrieval: vector search, then multi-hop traversal of ticket links (Graph-RAG).
  • Agent: a bilingual LangGraph agent sends each question to the graph, the live Jira, Confluence and GitLab APIs, or both in parallel.
  • Evaluation: every answer scored by RAGAS and an LLM judge, plus a deterministic validator that can veto the judge. Failure tags (hallucination, retrieval miss, tool misuse, ...) and GOLD / SILVER / BRONZE grades decide what may become training data.
  • Fine-tuning: RAFT (UC Berkeley's retrieval-augmented fine-tuning) with QLoRA adapters on Qwen3-8B, tracked in MLflow, served locally through Ollama.
  • Release gate: thresholds fixed before training; base model and adapter answer the same held-out questions and a deterministic scorer, not an LLM, decides.
